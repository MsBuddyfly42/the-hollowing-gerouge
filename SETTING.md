# Gerouge setting and architectural continuity

## Fictional premise

Gerouge is an expansive rural estate in St. Bernard Parish, Louisiana, imagined adjacent to the real Kenilworth Plantation. The large, eerie mansion is the primary location and spatial anchor. The former finishing school, missing pupils, Eva Grey, private records and sealed tower remain the existing investigation.

The game's founding lore credits Countess Elizabeth Báthory (1560–1614), the Blood Countess, as Gerouge's builder. This is an explicit fictional connection. It does not assert that the historical Báthory built a house in Louisiana, or associate the real Kenilworth property with Gerouge's crimes or supernatural events.

The mansion's visible architecture combines a Southern antebellum character with Italianate villa influences. Báthory's fictional founding and the later architectural appearance should not be presented as a single historically verified construction date. The broad 1783–1861 Southern period requested by the user provides context; Italianate details are principally a mid-nineteenth-century reference, not a style spanning that entire period.

## Spatial and visual requirements

- The mansion dominates the arrival scene and remains the return point for the evidence gathered outside.
- Keep the pale weathered exterior, deep galleries/verandahs, tall shuttered openings, low rooflines, projecting/decorative eaves, and distinctive tower consistent through future scenes. Italianate details should read as part of the same eclectic Southern house, not a replacement building.
- Show expansive rural grounds through garden paths, the conservatory, orchard route, gatehouse, carriage house/service grounds, school wing and chapel. Use existing route and evidence logic; do not remove progression locks.
- Preserve the mysterious photographic presentation and Gothic palette. Future scene plates should show these regional and architectural cues in physical detail, with interactions aligned to the actual scenery.
- The local travel map is schematic, not a geographic survey. Kenilworth is a setting reference, not a new playable destination.

## Implemented in the game

The approach identifies St. Bernard Parish, Louisiana. The plaque opens an estate dossier covering the mansion, Bathory founding lore, and the fictional adjacency to Kenilworth. Two architecture/grounds hotspots on the existing mansion plate open the corresponding dossier sections. The map includes the regional setting and a Mansion and grounds tab with existing playable destinations. Players can record this setting in their field journal. The original campaign's plantation-history entry now agrees with this setting.

No save keys, earned objectives, ending choices, mystery findings or inventory item identities were changed. The inventory remains 91 scene entries because the dossier and architectural inspections are new interactions in existing locations, not invented additional rooms.
